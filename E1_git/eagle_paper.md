# White-tailed eagle homework

## 1.What was the main research question?
Hansen et al. (2023) wanted to find out how genetically different the island and mainland populations of white-tailed eagles were. These eagles can fly long distances, so we might expect birds from different regions to meet and breed. The researchers still found clear genetic differences, especially between the island and mainland populations. This suggests that even though the birds can travel far, they do not breed across regions often enough to erase those differences.

## 2.Where did the samples come from?
The island samples came from Greenland and Iceland. The mainland samples came from Denmark, Norway and Estonia.

## 3.What are the historical samples, and why did the authors include them?
They were eagle specimens collected from 1885 to 1950, used to see how genetic diversity and population structure changed over time.


## 4.What data did the authors use, and how did they filter them?
Whole-genome shotgun sequencing of 92 eagles yielded 210,322 SNPs after filtering for quality, read depth (≥8), and missing data (≤25%). datadryad.org

## 5.Which analyses did they perform, and where are the results?
Diversity and inbreeding (Table 1, Figs. 2–3), \(F_{ST}\) (Table 2), PCA (Fig. 4), ADMIXTURE (Fig. 5), Tajima’s D (Table 3), and effective population size (Fig. 6). nsv.is

## 6.How did island and mainland populations differ?
Iceland and Greenland had lower genetic diversity and higher inbreeding than mainland populations. nsv.is

## 7.What four events may have contributed to the decline in effective population size?
Ice Age isolation, population divergence after the ice retreated, human expansion and settlement in Iceland, and recent persecution and pollution. nsv.is

## 8.What do these five terms mean?
- Founder effect — A few founders carry only part of the original population’s genetic variation.
- Genetic drift — Allele frequencies change by chance.
- Inbreeding — Related individuals mate.
- Philopatry — Individuals stay near or return to their birthplace to breed.
- Effective population size, the size of an ideal population that would experience the same amount of genetic drift.
